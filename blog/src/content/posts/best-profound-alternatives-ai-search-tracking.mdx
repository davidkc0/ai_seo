## What to look for in a Profound alternative

The mistake is comparing every feature line by line. Most teams do not need the biggest product. They need the fastest path to useful answers.

A good Profound alternative should tell you:

- whether ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews mention your brand
- which competitors appear instead
- whether the answer is positive, neutral, or negative
- which pages or citations may be influencing the result
- what to fix next on your site or content footprint

<Callout type="insight" title="The practical test">
  If the tool cannot explain what to fix after a scan, it is not an AI SEO tool. It is a very expensive screenshot machine.
</Callout>

## 1. Illusion

Illusion is built for the teams priced out of enterprise AI visibility.

## 2. Traditional SEO platforms

Some established SEO tools are adding AI search monitoring. That can work if your team already lives in those platforms and wants AI visibility alongside keyword research, backlinks, and technical SEO.

The tradeoff: AI search is often one feature inside a much larger SEO suite.

If the main question is “does AI recommend me and what should I fix?”, a focused tool can be cleaner.

---

## 3. Rank trackers with AI features

Rank trackers are good at recurring reports. Some are extending that muscle into AI answers.

That is useful for agencies and reporting-heavy teams. But the danger is treating AI answers like search result positions. AI responses are synthesized, variable, and citation-driven. You need recommendations, not only rows.

---

## 4. DIY AI search tracking

Manual checks are better than doing nothing.

Ask ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity the same buyer questions every week. Record whether you appear, who appears instead, and what the answer says.

The problem is variance. Models change responses, retrieval differs, and manual checks become annoying fast. Use DIY to validate that the channel matters. Use a tool when you need history and consistency.
